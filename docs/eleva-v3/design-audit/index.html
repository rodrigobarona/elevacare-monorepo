Method

How each slice is designed and judged

The same loop for every design challenge from now on. Mobbin sets the bar; the Eleva logo, palette, type and voice stay.

  1. Search Mobbin

    Search for the slice's problems and add what teaches something to _context/mobbin/library/{surface}/references.json.

  2. Capture before

    Run the capture spec if the surface changed since 2026-11-14-before.

  3. Design and build

    One slice, against the findings it closes and the references they cite.

  4. Capture after

    Same spec, new label. This page then shows before and after side by side.

  5. Judges panel

    Three judges score out of 10: UI and visual design; UX and UX writing; trust, legal, compliance and accessibility. Each reads the after captures against the surface's criteria with the references open.

  6. Pass or revise

    A slice ships only when every judge scores 8.5 or more and its P1 findings are closed. Otherwise it is revised and judged again. Anything marked for legal review also goes to a lawyer before launch.

01-homepage · Marketing and brand

Public homepage

The homepage looks calm and well made, and the headline says what Eleva is in one line, but it never shows a member a single expert they can book: the only named faces are advisors, and the route to a real expert is one generic button. Proof is thin (four two-word labels, no member voice, no numbers) and the page ends on a pitch to experts. The change that matters most is to put the choice of need and a row of real, bookable experts in the first two screens, and move the advisors below them as supporting proof.

Keep

  • The headline 'Women's health experts, on your schedule.' plus the lead line answers what Eleva is and who it is for in under ten seconds. Keep both.
  • Lora headings on cream with deep teal read as calm and premium, not clinical. The type scale and spacing are consistent from section to section.
  • The board of advisors is real proof: named people with credentials and a quote. Few health marketplaces at this stage have that.
  • 'No waiting lists and no account needed to book' is a strong, concrete promise, and the three steps are written in plain words.
  • One label, 'Find an expert', is used in the header, the hero and on mobile, so the main action is never renamed.

Findings

P1HOME-01uxNo bookable expert appears anywhere on the homepageD3.1

What is weak. The page shows eight advisors by name and face, and zero experts a member can book. A first-time visitor reads the advisor portraits as 'these are the experts', then the lead tells her 'Your care comes from the independent expert you choose', and the page never shows who that could be. Reaching one real person takes Find an expert, then the directory, then a profile. For a marketplace the supply is the product; hiding it makes the page feel like a brochure and is the main reason it reads as weak.

Do instead. Add a 'Meet our experts' row directly under the value strip: four to six real expert cards with photo, name, profession, languages, how they meet (video, phone, in person), price from, and the next free slot, each linking to the profile, plus 'See all experts'. On a phone make it a horizontal scroll so a card and a half is visible. Design the card for a new expert with no photo and a short bio (initials on a brand tint, profession and languages still carry the card). Rotate by category so every profession gets shown.
