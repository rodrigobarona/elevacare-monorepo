# Eleva public site: ten writing rules

Status: Living. Written by the UX writing seat of the research panel on 2026-11-23, from the
references saved in `_context/mobbin/library/08-marketing/` and `14-content-pages/`. The writing
judge of the public-site panel (`judges-panel.md`, seat 4) reads this page before scoring.

The founder's instruction for all copy: not verbose; calm; human first; natural and welcoming;
second person; plain words; say what Eleva does for the reader; "members", never "patients" or
"users".

Each rule is one line, with the saved references that support it, then a before and after. "Before"
is what the page says today (English strings in `apps/web/messages/en.json` and
`packages/content/content/pages/en/evidence-based-care.mdx`). "After" is a direction in Eleva's
voice, not final copy. Anything marked (confirm) is a fact the team must check before it ships.

---

**1. Give the hero a budget: a headline of eight words or fewer that names what the reader gets, and one lead of 20 words or fewer.**
References: 08-marketing/12 ClassPass, 14-content-pages/01 Calm, 14-content-pages/12 Metic.

- Before (/become-expert): "Practise on Eleva.care" and a five-line paragraph that begins "Eleva.care is a digital health platform for independent, licensed professionals."
- After: "Your practice, booked and paid in one place." Lead: "Set your own prices. Eleva keeps 15% of each booking, and nothing else."

**2. A sign-up asks only for an email, and says beside the field what arrives and how often.**
References: 14-content-pages/01 Calm, 14-content-pages/02 Tash.

- Before (/newsletter): "Essays, clinical context and community stories from the Eleva editorial team. Subscribe on Beehiiv—no Eleva account required."
- After: "Femme Focus is a letter on women's health from the experts on Eleva. It arrives twice a month (confirm). Leave your email and the next one is yours."

**3. A button names what happens next. No "Learn more", "Read more", "Get started", "Submit" or "here".**
References: 08-marketing/19 Employment Hero, 08-marketing/22 Mailchimp, 14-content-pages/05 Maze, 14-content-pages/12 Metic.

- Before: "Open community", "Subscribe on Beehiiv", "Open the help center".
- After: "Join the community", "Subscribe", "Find an answer". On /for-clinics: "Talk to us about your clinic".

**4. One idea per section, and each fact once per page.**
References: 08-marketing/14 Wise, 08-marketing/21 Headspace, 14-content-pages/07 Dropbox, 14-content-pages/17 Zendesk.

- Before (/podcast): the body says "new episodes announced in Femme Focus" and the link under it says "Get episode announcements in Femme Focus".
- After: the body is about the show only: "Conversations with the researchers and clinicians behind women's health." The newsletter is said once, as the link: "Tell me when a new episode is out".

**5. Give the number: a fee as a receipt, a reply time, an episode's length.**
References: 08-marketing/13 Unfair, 08-marketing/15 Railway, 14-content-pages/12 Metic, 14-content-pages/05 Maze.

- Before (/contact): "We read every message and reply in English, Portuguese or Spanish."
- After: "A person reads every message. We reply within two working days (confirm), in English, Portuguese or Spanish."
- Same rule on /become-expert: "Member pays 60 euros. Eleva keeps 9 euros. You receive 51 euros." (confirm with finance)

**6. Write each question as the reader would ask it, with "I" or "my", and answer it in the first sentence.**
References: 08-marketing/16 Acctual, 08-marketing/15 Railway.

- Before (/become-expert, inside the card "Your rates and policies"): "Your share reaches your Stripe account on the later of 7 days after payment or 24 hours after the session; Stripe then pays it to your bank."
- After: "When am I paid? Seven days after the member pays, or 24 hours after the session, whichever is later. Stripe then pays your bank."

**7. Start with the reader, not with Eleva. "You" comes before "we", and a worried reader is told there is no pressure.**
References: 14-content-pages/14 Bevel, 14-content-pages/17 Zendesk, 14-content-pages/13 Maxima Therapy.

- Before (/community): "Femme Focus community. Discuss episodes and newsletter topics with other members. The community lives on Beehiiv today; Eleva accounts are optional."
- After: "You're among women asking the same questions. Talk with other members about each issue and episode. No question is too small."

**8. Show the thing itself and caption it in a few words: a real issue, a real episode, a real screen.**
References: 14-content-pages/02 Tash, 14-content-pages/03 Webflow, 08-marketing/17 ISO Meet, 14-content-pages/16 Airbnb.

- Before (/newsletter): one paragraph that describes the newsletter, and no issue to look at.
- After: three past issues, each with its date, its own title, 25 words from the issue and "Read the issue". With no issues to show yet: "Past issues will appear here. The next one goes out soon." with "Subscribe".

**9. Plain words for what Eleva does. No internal, vendor or legal terms, and the people who book are members.**
References: 14-content-pages/09 Dovetail, 14-content-pages/08 Headspace, 14-content-pages/11 Tines, 08-marketing/16 Acctual.

- Before (/evidence-based-care): "Verified professional profiles and documentation checks where required for platform integrity." "Encrypted sessions and private storage for clinical artefacts."
- After: "We check each expert's registration with her professional body before her profile goes live. Your sessions are encrypted and never recorded." (the first sentence is on /become-expert today; confirm the second)
- Before (/contact): "exercising your rights under the GDPR". After: "seeing, correcting or deleting your data".

**10. People sign their words: first person, first names, short. The letter's headings tell the story when read alone.**
References: 08-marketing/20 KÖPPEN, 08-marketing/18 Airbnb, 14-content-pages/14 Bevel, 14-content-pages/15 Runway.

- Before (/history): lead "How a physiotherapist became a pioneer in evidence-based women's health." Headings "The academic journey", "Our mission". 619 words.
- After: lead "I'm Patrícia, a physiotherapist. I started Eleva because good answers were not reaching the women who needed them." Headings in her words: "I didn't have answers when the women I treated became pregnant", "The evidence existed. It wasn't reaching women.", "So we built Eleva". Signed "Patrícia and Rodrigo". On /about, the same story in 120 words or fewer, with "Read the full letter".

## Words Eleva leaves out

Seen on the reference sites and on today's pages, and worth a search before any page ships:
"platform integrity", "clinical artefacts", "modality", "standard plan", "operational owner",
"seat pricing", "inquiry", "departments", "pioneers", "dive into", "users", "patients"
(in Eleva's own voice; the founder may speak of her own clinical work in her letter, and legal
review decides that wording).

## Notes for whoever writes the copy

- The "after" lines carry facts that are not confirmed: how often Femme Focus goes out, the reply
  time, the worked fee example in euros, and what is said about session encryption. Each must be
  true on the day it ships, or the line changes. The 15% fee, the payout timing and the
  registration check are on /become-expert today.
- Vendor names: name a place only when the reader will land there and it helps her ("Listen on
  Spotify"). Do not name the tool behind a form ("Subscribe on Beehiiv").
- These pages are translated. Short sentences with one idea survive translation into Portuguese
  and Spanish; idioms do not.
